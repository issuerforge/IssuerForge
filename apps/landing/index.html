A stablecoin issuer's console on Solana

Carry out a regulator's order from your own wallets — and file the proof.

IssuerForge lets a fintech issue its own stablecoin on Solana with the compliance rules written into the token. When an order arrives, the officer freezes, seizes or pauses from the console; the issuer's quorum signs; and every action lands in a journal that anyone can check against the chain without asking us.

Part A

Every action names its basis and its case.

Freeze, seizure, pause and resume each carry one of twelve reason codes and a case reference. Without a basis the action is not taken. A frozen account can neither send nor receive, and frozen and seized balances are totalled apart from free circulation.

The code is a number on chain and a caption in the console. Renumbering would change what yesterday's actions mean, so codes are only ever added.

Part B

In force in seconds, measured from the holder's side.

The clock starts when the officer's request reaches the api and stops when a holder's next transfer is refused. A pump of transfers ran the whole time, so "in force" means a real transfer failed — not that a transaction confirmed.

The spread is the free public devnet node and its rate limits, not the program: the cleanest cycles took 1.4–2.0 s.

Part C

Money moves on two of your signatures. Never on ours.

A freeze needs the officer alone: it moves nothing, and the same officer can lift it. Seizure, pause, a policy change and a change of who may sign need two of the issuer's wallets, and the program counts them — not the console.

The platform runs an operational key for routine work — onboarding holders, updating their status — only within powers the issuer delegated, and the issuer revokes them in one action.

Part D

The policy sits in the token, and you can amend it after issue.

One shared program serves every issuer; each issuer's policy is data — verified recipients by tier, allowed jurisdictions, a cap per transfer, a cap per period, denied addresses. A refused transfer names the rule that refused it. Changing the policy is a quorum action; holders change nothing.

Part E

A stranger can reconcile the journal with the chain.

The journal exports a period as NDJSON. A separate verifier reads only that file and a Solana node — no api, no database of ours — and checks both ways: every line against a transaction, every transaction against a line.

Part F

Conditions of this return.

  1. Devnet only. No mainnet, no real money. The program is not audited.
  2. IssuerForge carries out obligations; it does not grant permission to issue. No licences, no filings.
  3. Holder verification, the sanctions list and the bank reserve are fixtures. The reserve attestor is our own key: the mechanism is real, the figures are made up.
  4. No further minting yet. The first issuance is checked against the attested reserve — 10 of 10 over-reserve attempts refused; a separate mint comes next.
  5. No public reserve page and no redemption into local currency yet.
  6. Amounts and addresses are public, like any token on Solana.
